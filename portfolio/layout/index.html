⚠ Real Story Cross-Hospital Referral
A German couple, pregnant after multiple miscarriages, needed a fetal cardiac echo at a public hospital our clinic couldn't provide.
In China's healthcare system, public hospitals offer specialized diagnostics that international private hospitals cannot. For foreign patients, transferring between hospitals means navigating a maze of Chinese-only interfaces, scattered departments, and unclear next steps — all during moments of high medical anxiety.

As a former patient service coordinator, I saw this firsthand. I had to translate, coordinate, and guide them through every step — because the system itself was not designed for them.

BridgeCare · Solution Architecture
Page 03
03 — Solution
Designing the Bridge
BridgeCare replaces the coordinator's memory with a structured task-chain engine, while keeping the coordinator in control of every critical decision.
Layer 1 · B2B
Desktop Care Platform
Kanban dashboard, task-chain management, template library, anomaly alerts, and guided booking — everything a coordinator needs.
Layer 2 · Engine
Task-Chain Engine
A rule-based engine that breaks each referral into dependent tasks with owners, SLAs, escalation rules, and fallback paths.
Layer 3 · B2C
Patient Mobile App
A minimal companion showing only the next action: today's appointment, documents to bring, route, and how to contact the coordinator.
System Architecture Diagram
🏗️
Three-Layer Architecture
Top: Desktop Care Platform (Kanban, Templates, Alerts, Booking). Middle: Task Chain Engine + Knowledge Base + Notification Engine + Integration Layer. Bottom: Patient App (Today, Timeline, Help). Draw data flow arrows between layers. Show integration types: API / Guided Manual / Structured Manual.
Task Chain Concept
🔗
Task Chain Visualization
Horizontal chain of 7-8 nodes: Translate Records → Book Appt → Send Prep → Day-Before Reminder → Check-in Nav → Collect Results → Trigger MDT. Color by owner: Purple=System, Blue=Coordinator, Green=Patient. Show dependency arrows + conditional branch (abnormal → MDT).
Why Kanban, not a list?
Kanban lets coordinators see workload distribution, bottlenecks, and anomalies at a glance across 40+ active patients.
Why templates, not free-form?
80% of referrals are predictable. Templates turn personal experience into institutional memory — new coordinators ramp up faster.
Why not full patient self-service?
Medical appointments require clinical judgment. The coordinator configures; the patient executes simple, trusted actions.

04 — Hard Turns
Designing Through Constraints
A good design isn't the one without tradeoffs — it's the one where the tradeoffs were made on purpose. Here's where I had to play defense.
"
When I first saw the data, my instinct was to build full automation — auto-booking, smart routing, the impressive demo. But Emma's rare-disease case and three hospitals with no open APIs changed my mind. I chose to give up the sexy direction to protect what actually matters: a system patients can trust.
Designer's Dilemma
The Impossible Triangle
1
The Automation Temptation
撞墙Target hospitals expose no booking APIs; one missed step means a stranded, anxious patient.
取舍Killed the "auto" demo. Chose guided human-in-the-loop: the system feeds the script, the human makes the call.
结果Guided Booking became the product's signature interaction — and earned coordinator trust.
2
The Template Trap
撞墙Emma (rare disease) broke every standard template; forcing her in dropped critical steps.
取舍Refused "standardize vs. flex" as either/or. Added a mandatory Customize layer after every template.
结果Templates became a starting point, not a cage — outliers stay safe.
3
The Scope Cut
撞墙Coordinators were already at capacity; a feature-rich patient app would only generate more incoming questions.
取舍Deliberately scoped the C-end down to one clear next step (Today / Timeline / Help).
结果Clean dual-role split: B-end thinks, C-end executes — a mature prioritization call.
✕ Gave Up
Full automation — the sexier direction, but untrustworthy in high-stakes care.
✓ Gained
Safety (zero dropped steps) + Personalization (fits every patient).
◆ Net
A system that keeps the human in the loop — and earns trust.

Reflections
1. Automation is a teammate, not a replacement.
In healthcare, trust is everything. The best automation handles reminders, routing, and status updates — and escalates to humans when ambiguity is high.
2. Design for the worst day.
A patient opening the app on the morning of a scary exam is not in a browsing mood. Every screen must reduce cognitive load, not add to it.
3. B2B tools have a B2C soul.
Even though the dashboard is for coordinators, every design decision ultimately affects a patient. The interface must feel calm, respectful, and human.
